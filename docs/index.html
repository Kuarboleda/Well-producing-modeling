Gas lift (continuous)

The lift gas is injected at one depth (valve or orifice). Above that depth the tubing carries the formation gas plus the lift gas, so the column is lighter.

The injection pressure is only used to check that the casing gas pressure at the injection depth is higher than the tubing pressure there. 0 = no check.

How to use this page
  1. Fill in the well on the left. It opens with a complete example well, so you can press Calculate straight away and change things afterwards. The four sections hold the reservoir and inflow model, the fluid, the equipment and well path, and gas lift.
  2. The VLP correlation chooses itself from the fluid, the water cut, the oil gravity and the well path, and the note under it says why. Pick a different one whenever you like; the app then stops choosing for you and offers a button back to its suggestion.
  3. Press Calculate. The inflow curve redraws as you type, but the tubing side takes about a third of a second, so it waits for the button. When an input changes afterwards, the answers go grey until you press it again.
  4. IPR / VLP and P-T profiles shows where the two curves cross — the rate the well flows at — and the pressure and temperature down the tubing at that rate.
  5. Sensitivity repeats the whole calculation for several values of one input, so you can see what a bigger tubing or a lower wellhead pressure is worth.
  6. VLP comparison and matching is where a measurement comes in. Enter a downhole gauge reading or a flowing gradient survey and the app shows how close each correlation comes, then matches the one you keep. No correlation is right until it is checked against something measured.

Save case writes the well to a small file on your computer, and Open case reads one back. The well you are working on is also remembered in this browser, so a refresh does not lose it; Reset puts everything back to the starting values.

        Matching bends the correlation until it reproduces the measurement, by multiplying its gravity and friction parts. It is worth doing when one correlation is already close; stretching a correlation that is far out usually means something else is wrong (the PVT, the rate, the water cut, the depths).
